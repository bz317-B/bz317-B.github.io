01 / FINANCE & INVESTMENT

Stock Investment

A self-directed stock portfolio that made investment decisions and performance measurement part of my everyday learning.

PROJECTPersonal investing
PERIODOct 2024 – Sep 2026
FOCUSPortfolio performance
01

The starting point

I wanted to apply my interest in finance to decisions with real outcomes. I managed a personal stock account and made approximately 100,000 in cumulative contributions over time, while keeping track of how the account performed.

02

What I did

  • Made my own buy and sell decisions and monitored the positions in my account.
  • Reviewed the profit and loss chart to see how the account changed across stronger and weaker periods.
  • Used the results to reflect on timing, uncertainty, and the importance of measuring performance carefully.
03

The result

For October 8, 2024 through September 30, 2026, the brokerage dashboard showed cumulative profit and loss of +28,097.68 and an account gain rate of +87.52%. These figures gave me a concrete record to evaluate, rather than an estimate of how my investments performed.

04

What I learned

An investment result is useful only when I understand how it is measured. This experience strengthened my habit of reviewing outcomes, questioning the numbers behind a headline return, and making decisions with risk in mind.

02 / OPERATIONS & RESEARCH

3D Printing & Supply Chain Design

A team research presentation on how additive manufacturing can affect production, inventory, and supply chain choices.

PROJECTTeam course presentation
ROLECase research & explanation
FOCUSSupply chain design
01

The question

Our team explored a practical business question: when a part can be produced through 3D printing, what might change elsewhere in the supply chain? We looked beyond the technology itself to the choices businesses make about sourcing, assembly, and inventory.

02

My contribution

  • Researched examples from aerospace and medical manufacturing to make the topic concrete.
  • Explained how design flexibility and part consolidation can change the number of components or steps in production.
  • Connected smaller production runs with possible inventory and lead time decisions, while recognizing that each application has different costs and quality requirements.
03

The presentation

We organized the findings into a clear comparison of potential benefits and tradeoffs. The goal was to help an audience see why a manufacturing choice can affect suppliers, production planning, and delivery—not just the shape of a product.

04

What I learned

New technology does not create value on its own. Its value depends on the full process around it: material choice, quality standards, production volume, cost, and the ability to deliver when needed.

Business lens

  • Design and part complexity
  • Supplier and assembly coordination
  • Inventory and lead time
  • Cost and quality tradeoffs

This is a summary of class research and presentation work. It does not claim measured savings or independently verified manufacturer results.

03 / LEADERSHIP & MANAGEMENT

Student Band Roadshow

Bringing school staff, sponsors, and performers together around a student band roadshow.

PROJECTStudent event
ROLEOrganizer
FOCUSCoordination & negotiation
01

The challenge

A band roadshow needs more than a performance plan. The event depended on communication with the school, conversations with potential sponsors, and coordination among performers. Each group had different priorities, so I needed to help turn the idea into a plan people could support.

02

My role

  • Communicated with school staff about the event and the support needed to organize it.
  • Approached sponsors and negotiated around their involvement and the resources available.
  • Coordinated with band members so the people involved understood the shared plan and their responsibilities.
03

The outcome

I organized the roadshow by keeping these conversations moving and aligning the groups involved. It was a hands-on lesson in managing an event where progress depended on other people's schedules, needs, and commitments.

04

What I learned

Leadership meant listening before making a request, explaining the purpose of the event clearly, and finding workable agreements. I became more comfortable managing several relationships while keeping a common goal in view.

Skills in action

  • School communication
  • Sponsor outreach and negotiation
  • Performer coordination
  • Event planning
